For the person who thinks their way through everything

You think you're choosing, but your story is.

You didn't choose it. You inherited it, or built it without noticing. Now it decides what feels possible before you ever weigh the options.

The only way to expose it is to write.

Ten minutes. A story set five years out. It shows up on the page where you can finally see it.

Engineers, founders, operators — people who trust a spreadsheet more than a story. Which is exactly why this works on them.

Your brain can't lie in a story the way it can in a plan

A plan stays in the lane you already approve of. You decide what goes in it. A story doesn't ask permission. Set it five years out and details show up you didn't choose to put there — and those are the ones worth reading.

"It's harder to lie in ten minutes of story than in a planning session that took three hours."

The analytical mind performs while the story reveals. That gap is the whole instrument.

Write it. See it. Move on it.

This is an early warning system

A radar sweep for the narrative you're already living inside. It catches the story shaping your decisions while you can still choose whether to keep building it — not a decade later when the options it closed are already gone.

This isn't a journaling app, and it won't make the decision for you.

It does one thing. It returns you to what you already know but can't reach by thinking — names it, and hands you the first move.

The unexamined life isn't a philosophy problem.
It's a ten-minute problem.
